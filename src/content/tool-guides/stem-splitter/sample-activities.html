Prepare a recording and share it with the class so students can listen without using their daily allowance for splitting recordings. To let them add notes, choose Class: listen and add notes and press Save sharing. Check the daily Listening Guy allowance in Settings before planning a chat activity.

1. One Stem at a Time

Choose a recording and split it into four stems. Play the first minute with all stems, then solo vocals and play it again. Ask students to describe what they hear in the singing.

Repeat with drums, bass, and other, then listen to bass and drums together. Ask students what they hear in other and have them pin a note at a moment they want to discuss. You can also choose six stems to include guitar and piano.

2. Mute the Melody

Choose a recording with a clear lead vocal. During the chorus, students mute vocals, try to hum the melody, and listen for it in the remaining stems. Then they listen to the voice alone and consider what helps them recognize the song.

Ask students whether the parts follow one melody or different melodies. They can also ask Listening Guy about the parts, then listen and pin a note about what they hear.

3. Find the Form

Give each group a recording in a folder that allows class notes. Students listen at full speed and mark sections they hear, such as the intro, verse, or chorus. At each change, they can note what they hear.

Groups read one another's notes and add a separate note where they disagree. Use [ and ] to revisit annotations during discussion. If a boundary is unclear, solo a part that changes there and listen again.

4. Ask, Then Verify

Use any split and reserve two Listening Guy turns per student for questions and a follow-up. Students choose one claim about an instrument or section from the opening guide, solo the relevant stem, find the passage, and press L to loop it.

Ask students to pin a note about what they hear and use it in a follow-up question. Listening Guy does not receive the recording or its waveforms, so its descriptions need to be checked by listening.

5. Slow Rhythm Lab

Choose a recording with a rhythm students can follow. Play drums at 0.5×, loop a short passage, and ask students to count the beats. Add bass and ask how it relates to the drums.

Return to 1× and listen again. If drums is quiet, listen for the rhythm in another stem.

Adapt the Format

For asynchronous work, start a discussion from classmates' notes. In a large lecture, lead One Stem at a Time or Slow Rhythm Lab as a live session, then assign Find the Form independently. Students can open a shared recording without using their allowance for splitting recordings.

Play and Move Through a Recording

Press the round play button or the space bar to play and pause all stems together. Drag the scrub handle or click a waveform to move to a passage. The left and right arrow keys move five seconds at a time.

Choose 0.5×, 0.75×, or 1× playback. Slower speeds preserve pitch, so a bass line stays in the same key. Slow a passage to count a rhythm or follow a phrase, then compare it at full speed.

Solo and Mute

Choose Mute to silence one stem. Solo silences the others and changes the chosen stem's button to Only. You can solo several stems, such as vocals and drums, to compare their relationship before returning to the full mix.

During independent listening, your mix stays local to your browser. In a live session, the instructor sends an instrument focus, but your own mute switches still apply.

Repeat a Passage

Press L to loop the current region and listen again to a riff, cadence, or drum fill. Press Esc to clear both the loop and any solo.

Stem Labels and Colors

StemWhat the model places here
VocalsLead and backing voices. Spoken word and crowd noise usually land here too.
DrumsDrum kit and most percussion.
BassElectric and upright bass, low synth bass.
OtherHorns, strings, organ, synths, and other sounds the model leaves outside the named stems.
GuitarSix-stem only. Acoustic and electric guitar.
PianoSix-stem only. Piano and some keyboard parts.

In a two-stem split, Instrumental holds the accompaniment. Courses can rename labels to match the sounds students hear. For example, a class might call Other “fiddle” after checking its contents. See Class Workspace for course settings.

Check What the Split Contains

Labels are not guarantees. Other may contain reverb, room sound, brass, and reeds on the same track. Older recordings with surface noise or a narrow mono mix may leave hiss in Vocals and little sound in Drums.

Even a six-stem split may spread a piano chord across Piano and Other or leave guitar in another stem. Compare isolated parts with the full recording before naming an instrument. Sample Activities helps students describe both what they hear and where separation makes listening harder.

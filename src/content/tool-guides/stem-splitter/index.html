About Stem Splitter

Stem Splitter uses Demucs to separate a recording into stems such as vocals, drums, and bass. The stems play together in a browser mixer, where you can mute a part or hear it alone. Start by describing what you hear, then compare the isolated part with the full recording. You do not need formal music training to begin.

Stem Splitter was developed as part of the tinkering track of the Critical AI Literacy Institute from spring 2026 through fall 2026. Ethnomusicology professor Agustina Checa created the tool for music teaching, and the CUNY AI Lab hosts it.

The Listening Guy guide and chat can suggest questions and operate the mixer, but the model cannot hear the audio. Use the stems and your timestamped notes to check its claims.

Listen, Note, and Share

Use The Mixer to hear a stem alone, slow a passage, or repeat it in a loop. Notes shows how to mark a passage and describe what changed. Then try a question in Listening Guy and check the answer by listening. Export & Sharing covers downloads, public audio links, and course folders.

Bring a Class In

Instructors need a course in the Lab's admission system to use classroom features. Start with Class Workspace to assign work and share folders, then use Student Onboarding to guide the first session. You can lead playback through Live Sessions and set teaching instructions in the Instructor Console. For listening tasks, see Teaching Tips, Sample Activities, and the demonstrated workflow in Use Cases.

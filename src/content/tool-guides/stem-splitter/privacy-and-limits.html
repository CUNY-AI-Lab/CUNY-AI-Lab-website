On the Splitter tab, you can choose a course under Share with. Check its acknowledgment before creating work for that course. Earlier work stays under Personal. Instructors can read course conversations without generating answers, editing, or forwarding them. Classmates need current membership and an instructor's folder permission. For class notes, choose Class: listen and add notes, then choose Save sharing.

Retention and Deletion

The app deletes uploaded audio and stems 90 days after a split, and public listening links expire at the same time. A class folder can still show an expired split, but it no longer provides its audio.

Download any stems you need before deleting a split or reaching its retention limit. Choosing Delete hides the split and blocks further access after the server accepts the request. The app removes private content after any active processing finishes, while preserving audio still used by another split.

Daily Allowances

For signed-in members, Account → Dashboard shows how many recordings you can split and how many Listening Guy turns you have left today, along with the reset time. Both reset at midnight UTC, which is 8:00 PM Eastern during daylight time and 7:00 PM during standard time. Successfully splitting a recording counts toward your allowance, and failed or cancelled attempts do not. Reading existing guides and conversations does not use a turn. The dashboard also estimates the separate model allowance shared across CUNY AI Lab tools.

Sources and Licensing

Uploads can be MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, OGG, or AIFF files up to 100 MB. Use music you are entitled to use for class. Internet Archive search returns public, downloadable MP3s with music metadata and a credited source. The app's duration limit allows verified students and instructors more time than visitors. Metadata alone does not establish reuse permission.

If you paste a YouTube link, the app looks up the video's title and offers to search the Internet Archive. It does not retrieve audio from YouTube, including when you are signed in to YouTube.

The search field combines results from Internet Archive, Wikimedia Commons, Freesound, and Library of Congress. Results identify the source and show license details when available. Check the source recording's rights before importing or sharing it. Supported imports retain source credits, including in exports. The Export ZIP can also include your notes and Listening Guy conversation, so review it before sharing.

Guest Access

Visitors can split recordings as guests and try a Listening Guy demo. Guests can complete five splits per UTC day. Guest work stays in the browser where it was created and follows the same retention rules. After signing in, visitors can claim their work into an account. Course features require a signed-in member.

The guest Listening Guy demo allows two successful replies for the entire browser session. Generating a guide and receiving chat replies count toward that total across recordings, tabs, reloads, resets, and UTC days. Failed, empty, cancelled, or incomplete replies do not count toward that limit. The third request asks the visitor to sign in before generating another reply.

Report a Problem

Choose Report a bug in the footer and describe the problem. Use CUNY Login, or choose No account? and enter your first name, last name, and a .edu email address. Signed-in identity is verified, and guest contact details are self-reported and grant no app access. Reports include your description, the page, and the app release, but do not automatically attach audio, notes, or conversations.

The app stores reports and delivery records privately for support. It sends reports to Redmine when available and may use email if Redmine is not configured or rejects a report. An Email accepted for delivery message means the mail service accepted the report and does not confirm delivery to a mailbox. For access questions, email ailab@gc.cuny.edu.

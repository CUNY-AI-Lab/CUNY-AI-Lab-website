Prepare the Recording

Split the repertoire and organize recordings in shared folders. Check the source's license and audio quality before sharing. Use read-only access for demonstrations, or allow class notes when students will annotate.

Listen before assigning stem labels. If Other mainly holds a banjo, rename it in Class settings so students share a useful term, while still acknowledging any other sounds mixed into it. Put the vocabulary you want Listening Guy to use in the Instructor Console. Choose concepts students can test, such as whether parts carry independent lines.

Make One Change and Listen Again

Play a passage with all stems, mute one layer, and replay it. Ask students what changed before introducing a definition. For a quick rhythm, try 0.5×, count it together, then return to full speed to check the pattern.

During a live session, project the mixer so students can connect controls with the sound in their headphones. Pin a note directing their next listen. The demonstration's vocals-and-drums comparison gives them two parts to follow.

Check Listening Guy's Account

Listening Guy cannot hear the audio. Ask students to identify a stem and timestamp supporting or challenging each claim, rather than accepting a plausible genre description. Plan within the daily turn allowance; two or three questions leave room for a follow-up.

Read course conversations through the roster to see where students need help. If a term causes confusion, return to a passage where the class can hear the distinction.

Review the Evidence

Ask for timestamped notes naming the soloed stems, mixer state, and audible change. Listen to that passage when assessing the response, and ask for revision if the evidence remains unclear.

In a folder that allows class notes, students can compare section boundaries and explain disagreements. Revisit those passages together before deciding where a bridge or chorus begins.

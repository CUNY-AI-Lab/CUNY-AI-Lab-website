Prepare the Recording

Split the repertoire and organize recordings in shared folders. Check the source's license and audio quality before sharing. Use read-only access for demonstrations, or allow class notes when students will annotate.

You can rename a split in the Session Rack. Instructors can also change labels in Class settings. Listen to a recording before changing a label, since a stem may contain more than one sound.

Make One Change and Listen Again

Play a passage with all stems, mute one layer, and replay it. Ask students what changed before introducing a definition. For a quick rhythm, try 0.5×, count it together, then return to full speed to check the pattern.

During a live session, project the mixer so students can see which controls affect the sound. In the demonstration, students listened to vocals and drums together.

Check Listening Guy's Account

Listening Guy does not receive the recording or its waveforms. Ask students to listen and use their notes to check its descriptions. See Listening Guy for what the model receives.

Read course conversations through the roster to see where students need help. If a term causes confusion, return to a passage where the class can hear the distinction.

Review the Evidence

Ask students to pin notes about what they hear. During discussion, select a note's timestamp to listen to the passage together.

In a folder that allows class notes, students can compare section boundaries and explain disagreements. Revisit those passages together before deciding where a bridge or chorus begins.

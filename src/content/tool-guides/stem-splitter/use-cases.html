The “Stardust” example below comes from the CAIL Teaching Tools demonstration. The adaptations that follow use the same listening, sharing, and annotation controls with your own course recordings.

Listen to vocals and drums

In the demonstration, the instructor split Willie Nelson's “Stardust” into six stems. A note at 1:10 asked students to listen to vocals and drums together, then try vocals with another stem. Listening Guy could read the note.

Listening Guy could mute the other stems, but it could not identify an exact moment in the recording. The students listened to find it themselves.

To try the comparison, play the complete recording first and describe the voice and drums before asking the model. Hear those parts alone, loop a passage with L, and pin a note explaining what you hear. Try 0.75× if needed, then return to the full mix. If a stem contains bleed or an unexpected instrument, include that in the observation rather than assuming the label is correct.

Share a Recording for Notes

Prepare a recording and add it to a course folder that lets class members add notes. Students can open the recording without using their allowance for splitting recordings. Ask students to listen and pin notes about what they hear. One Stem at a Time offers a short activity.

During discussion, click a note's timestamp to return to the passage. Play it for the class and ask students what they hear and whether they agree with the note.

Ask, Then Verify

After students listen, ask them to choose a claim from Listening Guy's guide and find the passage it describes. They can pin a note about what they hear and ask a follow-up question. Listening Guy does not receive the recording or its waveforms, so students should check its descriptions by listening.

For a group activity, lead a live session on a shared course recording. Press Start shared session; students open that recording and choose Follow instructor. Pause at a note and allow a few seconds for everyone to reach the passage. If audio is blocked, they can press Join shared audio. Ask students to keep the app visible, since a hidden tab pauses their audio. They can still use their own mute switches or leave the session to listen independently.

Keep Access and Retention in View

Students choose a course under Share with and acknowledge instructor review before creating new course work. Personal experiments can remain under Personal. Instructors control classmates' access through folder permissions; read-only access suits a demonstration, while note-taking needs the annotation permission. Folders do not extend a recording's retention period; download any stems you need before they expire. For more short tasks, see Sample Activities.

The “Stardust” example below comes from the CAIL Teaching Tools demonstration. The adaptations that follow use the same listening, sharing, and annotation controls with your own course recordings.

Compare Vocals and Drums

In the demonstration, the instructor used a six-stem split of Willie Nelson's “Stardust.” A note at 1:10 asked students to hear vocals and drums alone, then compare vocals with another stem. Listening Guy could read that note without an added teaching instruction.

When asked where the parts sounded most in sync, Listening Guy muted bass, other, and guitar but acknowledged that it could not locate an exact moment from the audio. The mixer action narrowed the comparison; listeners still had to find the passage and describe the timing.

To try the comparison, play the complete recording first and describe the voice and drums before asking the model. Hear those parts alone, loop a passage with L, and pin a note explaining what you hear. Try 0.75× if needed, then return to the full mix. If a stem contains bleed or an unexpected instrument, include that in the observation rather than assuming the label is correct.

Adapt This Workflow: Share a Recording for Notes

Prepare a course split in a folder that lets class members add notes. Students can open an already split recording without spending a split run. Ask each student to name a sound before checking it in isolation, then pin a timestamped description. One Stem at a Time gives a short sequence to follow.

Use [ and ] to revisit notes during discussion. Compare the proposed instrument or section boundary with the full recording. Other may hold several instruments, and a quiet or empty stem does not prove an instrument is absent. If you want shared vocabulary, add a focused instruction through the Instructor Console.

Adapt This Workflow: Ask, Then Verify

After students have made their own observations, ask them to choose one claim from Listening Guy's guide. They should find the passage, try the relevant mixer action, and note whether the sound supports the claim. Reserve two turns per student so they can ask a question and follow up with their evidence. The model cannot hear the recording; its description is something to test, not an account of what it has heard.

For a group comparison, lead a live session on the shared course recording. Press Start shared session; students open that recording and choose Follow instructor. Pause at a note and allow a few seconds for followers to reach the passage. If audio is blocked, they can press Join shared audio. Keep the app visible: hidden follower tabs pause audio. Students' own mute switches still apply, and they can leave the session to listen independently.

Keep Access and Retention in View

Students choose a course under Share with and acknowledge instructor review before creating new course work. Personal experiments can remain under Personal. Instructors control classmates' access through folder permissions; read-only access suits a demonstration, while note-taking needs the annotation permission. Folders do not extend a recording's retention period; download any stems you need before they expire. For more short tasks, see Sample Activities.

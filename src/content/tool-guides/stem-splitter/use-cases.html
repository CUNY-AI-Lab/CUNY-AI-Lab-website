The “Stardust” example below comes from the CAIL Teaching Tools demonstration. The examples that follow suggest ways to listen, share recordings, and make notes with your own class.

Listen to vocals and drums

In the demonstration, the instructor split Willie Nelson's “Stardust” into six stems. A note at 1:10 asked students to listen to vocals and drums together, then try vocals with another stem. Listening Guy could read the note.

Listening Guy muted the other stems, but it could not identify an exact moment in the recording, so the students listened to find it themselves.

The mixer lets students listen to parts on their own, slow down a passage, and add notes at moments they want to return to. A stem can include sounds besides the ones named in its label.

Share a Recording for Notes

Prepare a recording and add it to a course folder that lets class members add notes. Students can open the recording without using their allowance for splitting recordings. They can listen and pin notes about what they hear. The Sample Activities offer more ideas for working with a recording.

During discussion, click a note's timestamp to return to the passage. Play it for the class and ask students what they hear and whether they agree with the note.

Ask, Then Verify

After listening, students can ask Listening Guy about the recording and use their notes to consider its response. Listening Guy does not receive the recording or its waveforms, so its descriptions need to be checked by listening.

For a group activity, lead a live session on a shared course recording. Press Start shared session; students open that recording and choose Follow instructor. Pause at a note and allow a few seconds for everyone to reach the passage. If audio is blocked, they can press Join shared audio. Ask students to keep the app visible, since a hidden tab pauses their audio. They can still use their own mute switches or leave the session to listen independently.

Keep Access and Retention in View

Students choose a course under Share with and acknowledge instructor review before creating new course work. They can keep other work under Personal. Instructors choose whether classmates can only listen and read notes or also add notes. Shared folders do not extend the 90-day retention period, so download any stems you need before they expire. For more short tasks, see Sample Activities. The Lab also provides Sandbox Docs for its companion tool.

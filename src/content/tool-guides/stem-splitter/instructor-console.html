Check the Scope

Open the Console from Settings or the footer link. Before editing, check the scope notice. A course Console affects that course's work, while the application-wide Console affects non-course work. Neither setting is private to your instructor account. Each scope keeps its own revision history.

Save a Teaching Instruction

Write in Teaching instructions as you would brief a tutor. You can name the concept, the students' experience level, and a useful next step. The field accepts up to 2,000 characters. The app adds it to the fixed system prompt for each guide and answer; that base prompt stays read-only.

Add the required change note, up to 240 characters, explaining what changed and why, then save. New answers use the instruction immediately. An older guide regenerates when next requested after a prompt change. To reset the instruction, clear the field and save with a change note. Use Preview saved prompt to see the assembled prompt sent to the model. History under Prompt reference keeps each revision and its change note.

Connect a Concept to Listening

The demo course introduces monophony, or one melodic line, and polyphony, or independent lines sounding together. Students can listen to the recording to consider how the parts relate. Listening Guy does not receive the recording or its waveforms.

Start with one concept and ask Listening Guy to explain it in language your students can use. You can also ask it to say when it is unsure. Read saved conversations to see how the instruction is working.

Choose Search Examples

Use Search examples to set the songs suggested beneath the Internet Archive search field on the Splitter tab. Check each recording's audio quality and your permission to use it before suggesting it to students.

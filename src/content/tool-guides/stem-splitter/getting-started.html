Get Access

Your instructor first requests a course through the CUNY AI Lab. Once the Lab approves and activates the course, accept your instructor's invitation while it is open. You can then open Stem Splitter under My classes in the Lab portal.

Open Stem Splitter from My classes or sign in at stem-splitter.ailab-452.workers.dev. The app recognizes your course role. Lab members without a course can also sign in and work privately in their accounts.

Sign In

Choose Sign in and use your usual CUNY Login, including any password or multi-factor prompt. Use a recent browser with audio playback, such as Chrome, Firefox, Safari, or Edge. Headphones help you hear quieter parts without disturbing others. After signing in, open the Splitter tab. Your course work appears in Workspace. Settings shows how many recordings you can split and how many Listening Guy turns you have left today.

Split Your First Recording

If you open the Splitter tab directly, Personal is selected under Share with, and you can practice privately. If you choose Create a split in Class Workspace, that class is selected instead. Before creating work for the course, read and check the acknowledgment described in Class Workspace.

Drop a file onto Insert Track, or click the drop zone to browse. The app accepts MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, OGG, and AIFF files up to 100 MB. Use music you are entitled to use for class.

To find audio online, enter a title under Search for songs via Internet Archive or Wikimedia Commons, choose a provider under Audio source, select a verified result, and choose Fetch. Archive results are public, downloadable MP3s that pass duration and metadata checks. Commons results have verified public-domain, CC0, or CC BY recording rights, and their creator, license, and source credits stay with the recording and its exports.

Unless you have saved another choice, the app selects Auto to choose how to split the recording. Depending on the file and app settings, Auto may use a default when analysis is unavailable. You can also choose to separate it into two, four, or six stems. Four separates vocals, drums, bass, and other, and six adds guitar and piano. Two separates vocals from the instrumental accompaniment. A stem may contain more than its label suggests, so listen to what is there.

Watch the new entry in the Session Rack. When its badge reads READY, press play to hear the stems together. Splitting a typical song may take a minute or two. You can leave the page and return to check its progress.

Listen and Make Notes

Once the recording is ready, try listening to a stem on its own or muting it while the others play. You can pause, slow down, or return to a passage, and add a note when you hear something you want to remember. See Splitter Basics for the Session Rack and mixer controls.

You can also ask Listening Guy a question. Listening Guy does not receive the recording or its waveforms, so listen to check its descriptions. Course work is subject to the review policy you acknowledge, and the app deletes uploaded audio and stems 90 days after a split. See Privacy & Limits for details.

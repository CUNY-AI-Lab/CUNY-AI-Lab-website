Get Access

For course work, your instructor first requests a course through the CUNY AI Lab's course application. Once the Lab approves and activates it, Stem Splitter appears under My classes in the Lab portal. Accept your instructor's class invitation while its invitation window is open.

Open Stem Splitter from My classes or sign in at stem-splitter.ailab-452.workers.dev. The app recognizes your course role. Lab members without a course can also sign in and keep personal splits private to their accounts.

Sign In

Choose Sign in and use your usual CUNY Login, including any password or multi-factor prompt. Use a recent browser with audio playback, such as Chrome, Firefox, Safari, or Edge. Headphones help you hear quieter parts without disturbing others. After signing in, open the Splitter tab. Workspace holds course work; Settings shows how many split runs and Listening Guy turns remain today.

Make a First Split

Leave Share with set to Personal while you learn the controls. Your recording will stay private to your account. Before creating course work, read the policy and acknowledgment in Class Workspace.

Drop a file onto Insert Track, or click the drop zone to browse. The app accepts MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, OGG, and AIFF files up to 100 MB. Use music you are entitled to use for class.

To find audio online, enter a title under Search for songs via Internet Archive or Wikimedia Commons, choose a provider with Audio source, select a verified result, and press Fetch. Archive results are public, downloadable MP3s that pass duration and metadata checks. Commons results have verified public-domain, CC0, or CC BY recording rights; their creator, license, and source credits stay with the recording and its exports.

A new session selects Auto unless you have saved another valid choice. Auto analyzes the imported audio before choosing a split. You can instead choose four stems to compare vocals, drums, bass, and other sounds. Two stems give you vocals and an instrumental accompaniment; six add guitar and piano. Extra stems may contain other instruments, so listen before relying on their labels.

Watch the new entry in the Session Rack. When its badge reads READY, press play to hear the stems together. For a typical song, GPU processing may take a minute or two; you can leave the page and return to check progress.

Try a Listening Comparison

Solo the bass for one minute. Clear the solo and replay that minute with every stem, then mute the bass and describe what changed. Splitter Basics explains the rack and mixer controls.

Next, take a claim from Listening Guy, hear the relevant stem, and pin a note where the sound supports or challenges it. The model cannot hear the audio. Course work is subject to the review policy you acknowledge, and the app deletes uploaded audio and stems after 90 days. See Privacy & Limits before sharing or relying on the app to keep your work.

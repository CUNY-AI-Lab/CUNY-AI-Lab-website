Prepare One Memo and Four Questions

A short memo that everyone reads can offer students a shared starting point for discussing the people, organizations, resources, and policies they choose to map.

Students must answer a Field Notes question to complete each level, so prepare a question for each one in the Instructor Console. For example, you might ask which people or settings are missing at Micro and what students want to investigate at Meso. You can add your course's definitions under Guidance and materials for Part 1 and Part 2, then review the suggestions before assigning the memo.

Walk Through One Decision

Project a suggestion and open its source passage before choosing Add to map, Set aside, or Remove. Ask what the passage establishes and what the student or model has inferred. At Micro, inspect the evidence for an item. At Meso, switch to List to inspect a relationship's verb, direction, and evidence.

A hospital and its billing office can help explain where items belong. Ask where the person in the memo participates and which decisions affect them indirectly. Use the answer to decide whether to place an item at Micro or Exo.

Question the Model's Reading

At Micro, the model can suggest up to ten items, though it may suggest fewer or none. It reads the material provided to it. Once course guidance is ready, the app reads the memo once for each new level without work, and existing saved work opens without another reading. Later readings are optional, and saving manual work does not request another one.

Students can return to the memo and their Field Notes when they are unsure about a connection. Ask them what the evidence supports and what they still need to find out.

Read Students' Maps

If you want to track progress, ask students to save their work and choose Submit revision after each level. Submit revision does not send the map, so ask for a project link or an exported copy when you need to read it. Exported copies are fixed versions you can compare.

When you read a map, consider whether its source passages support the claims students make and whether they have identified uncertainty, counterevidence, or missing perspectives. Their Field Notes can also help you discuss what they want to investigate next.

Choose Sources That Work Together

The first memo provides local people, settings, and resources. The second adds outside settings and broader conditions relevant to the same situation. Make their relationship clear in the assignment, and check that both sources contain passages students can use as evidence.

Model One Interpretation

Select a node, read its quotation, and discuss its label and classification. Then draw a connection and explain its direction and wording. Students make all relationships, so allow time for them to work out what they want each line to say.

Use Classification as a Question

Ask how the focal people participate in a setting and how an influence reaches them. A service provider can be local when students work with it directly. An outside decision setting can be Exo when its actions affect them indirectly. A policy or structural condition belongs to a different kind of analysis from the office that administers it.

Leave Room for Missing Evidence

The app may add fewer than six nodes or none at all. Check whether the memo supports the proposed placement before asking for more output. Students can add supported nodes manually and explain uncertainty in their interpretations or optional Field Notes.

Read the Work

Ask for a project link or exported copy when you need to read a student's map. Course progress records can help you see whether work has been saved, while the shared map provides its evidence and interpretation. Make your own assignment expectations clear, including whether you want Field Notes.

Prepare One Memo and Four Questions

Start with a shared case of about a page. The fictional East Harlem care-coordination case and library-and-garden example offer starting points; neither replaces your course's reading or fieldwork. A common memo lets students trace disagreements to the same source.

Write the four Field Notes questions before preparing model guidance. Students must answer a question to complete each level. At Micro, ask which person or setting is missing and which relationship they want to investigate at Meso. Paste the course's definitions into Guidance and materials for Part 1 and Part 2, then inspect a set of suggestions before assigning the memo.

Walk Through One Decision

Project a suggestion and open its source passage before choosing Add to map, Set aside, or Remove. Ask what the passage establishes and what the student or model has inferred. At Micro, inspect the evidence for an item. At Meso, switch to List to inspect a relationship's verb, direction, and evidence.

A hospital and its billing office can help explain placement. Ask where the focal person participates and which decisions reach them indirectly. The answer, not the institution's name, determines whether it belongs at Micro or Exo.

Keep the Model's Reading Open to Question

A Micro review can suggest up to ten items and no relationships, or return fewer items or none. It knows only the supplied material, not the field site. An empty map and a new level without work require their initial reading; later rereading is optional, and saving manual work makes no model request.

Students can use Map Check on an uncertain connection. It returns at most four notes. Compare them with the evidence before revising, and keep one unsupported suggestion or mistaken placement for discussion.

Read the Work, Not Just the Progress Record

Ask students to record a saved revision after each level if you want to track progress. To read the map, request an eligible project link or exported copy. Exports give you fixed versions to compare; revision counts do not contain the work.

Assess whether the source passage supports the claim and whether uncertainty, counterevidence, and missing perspectives qualify it. Use Field Notes to frame feedback around one gap and one next observation, then look for that inquiry in the student's next version.

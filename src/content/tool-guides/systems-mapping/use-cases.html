Use these examples with your course's readings and questions. Students begin with a memo, build a map, and write Field Notes about what they notice.

Start with a Shared Memo

Give students a short memo and publish Field Notes questions and Part 1 guidance. One fictional example follows Rosa as she receives care at Metropolitan Hospital and is referred to a neighborhood center. Another considers a resident's access to a library and a garden.

At Micro, students review the suggestions and add items supported by the memo. At Meso, they connect those items. In Rosa's case, a nurse refers her to a neighborhood center. Students can name that relationship, choose the direction of the arrow, and add the relevant passage from the memo.

Use Field Notes to ask what students still want to find out. If they add a billing office at Micro, ask whether Rosa participates there or whether its decisions affect her indirectly. That question can help them decide whether to place it at Exo.

Return to the Map After Fieldwork

Use a de-identified note, observation, or interview as the next memo. Follow your placement's confidentiality rules and check what the text reveals even after names change. The app stores the memo and sends it to the model when you request guidance. Check the audience before sharing an export.

Ask students to map what the note supports and use Field Notes to name one gap and one observation for the next visit. A conference or referral may support a Meso relationship between existing people and settings. A volunteer's work can connect a person to both a library and a garden; the map should show the resident's access, not simply list every neighborhood asset.

At the next visit, students compare their plan with what they observed, revise the evidence, and explain what changed. Each new level without work requires its initial reading. Once that reading is complete, they can revise manually; another model pass is optional.

Trace an Outside Decision and a Broader Condition

Start from a completed Part 1 map and add a short reading about an institution or policy. At Exo, identify an office that makes a decision affecting the person in the memo. Show how the decision reaches a setting at Micro, using the reading to explain any steps in between.

At Macro, add the broader condition and connect it to at least two earlier items. Explain the connections using evidence from the reading. Students can use Check this connection at Exo and compare the model's notes with the source before revising.

Read and Respond to the Work

Ask for a project link to read the live map, or an exported copy when you need a fixed version. Students can also use Submit revision to record their progress, but the record does not include the map. Use the shared evidence and Field Notes to frame a question for the next revision.

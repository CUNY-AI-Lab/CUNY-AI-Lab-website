These examples show how students might use a memo to build a map and write Field Notes about what they notice. You can adapt them to your course's readings and questions.

Start with a Shared Memo

Give students a short memo and publish Field Notes questions and Part 1 guidance. One fictional example follows Rosa as she receives care at Metropolitan Hospital and is referred to a neighborhood center. Another considers a resident's access to a library and a garden.

At Micro, students review the suggestions and add items supported by the memo. At Meso, they connect those items. In Rosa's case, a nurse refers her to a neighborhood center. Students can name that relationship, choose the direction of the arrow, and add the relevant passage from the memo.

Use Field Notes to ask what students still want to find out. If they add a billing office at Micro, ask whether Rosa participates there or whether its decisions affect her indirectly. That question can help them decide whether to place it at Exo.

Return to the Map After Fieldwork

Use a de-identified note, observation, or interview as the next memo. Follow your placement's confidentiality rules and check what the text reveals even after names change. The app stores the memo and sends it to the model when you request guidance. Check the downloaded file before sharing it.

Students can map what the note supports and use Field Notes to record what they still want to learn. For example, a conference or referral may support a Meso relationship between people and settings already on the map, while a volunteer's work might connect a resident to a library and a garden.

At the next visit, students compare their plan with what they observed, revise the evidence, and explain what changed. For a new level without work, the app must first read the memo once course guidance is ready. After that reading, students can revise the map themselves and choose whether to request more suggestions.

Trace an Outside Decision and a Broader Condition

Start from a completed Part 1 map and add a short reading about an institution or policy. At Exo, identify an office, policy, or other larger-scale item that affects the person in the memo, and record what the reading says about it.

At Macro, students can connect those larger-scale items to one another and to the local map. They can use the reading to explain each connection and decide what they still need to investigate.

Read and Respond to the Work

Ask students for a project link to read the live map, or an exported copy when you need a fixed version. They can also use Submit revision to record their progress, but that record does not include the map. Use the shared evidence and Field Notes to frame a question for the next revision.

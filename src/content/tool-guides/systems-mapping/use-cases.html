Use these three workflows with your course's sources and questions. Each follows the instructor-guided memo → map → Field Notes assignment, with time to test an interpretation and return to the source.

Start with a Shared Memo

Give students a short common memo, name the focal person in the inquiry, and publish Field Notes questions and Part 1 guidance. The fictional East Harlem case offers one example: Rosa receives care at Metropolitan Hospital, Nurse Jo refers her to a neighborhood center, and paperwork connects the settings. The library-and-garden example offers another, centered on a resident's access to community resources.

At Micro, students review the initial guidance, decide which items the source supports, and record evidence. Ask what person, resource, or setting is missing. At Meso, they connect existing items: Nurse Jo refers Rosa, Marisol accompanies Rosa, or the hospital sends paperwork to the center. Each relationship needs a meaningful phrase, a direction supported by the memo, and an evidence record. Do not create a new mesosystem node.

Use Field Notes to ask what the map still cannot explain. If a billing office appears at Micro, return to Rosa's participation: an outside decision that affects her indirectly belongs at Exo. A shared source makes these placement decisions easier to discuss.

Return to the Map After Fieldwork

Use a de-identified process note, observation, or interview as the next memo. Follow the placement's confidentiality rules and inspect what the text reveals even after names change. The app stores the memo, and model review sends the supplied material; problem reports exclude it. Check the audience before sharing an export.

Ask students to map what the note supports and use Field Notes to name one gap and one observation for the next visit. A conference or referral may support a Meso relationship between existing people and settings. A volunteer's work can connect a person to both a library and a garden; the map should show the resident's access, not simply list every neighborhood asset.

At the next visit, students compare their plan with what they observed, revise the evidence, and explain what changed. Each new level without work requires its initial reading. Once that reading is complete, they can revise manually; another model pass is optional.

Trace an Outside Decision and a Broader Condition

Start from a completed Part 1 map and add a short institutional or policy reading. At Exo, identify the office making a decision without the focal person's participation, then trace the effect into a Micro setting. Include a supported intermediate office when needed rather than drawing an unexplained arrow from a policy to a person.

At Macro, add the broader condition and scope it to at least two earlier items. Explain each scope with evidence and qualify uncertainty or counterevidence. Students can use Check this connection on an Exo pathway, compare the notes with the source, then export Markdown as the basis for a case-grounded policy explanation.

Read and Respond to the Work

Ask for an eligible project link to read the live map, or an exported copy when you need a fixed version for comparison or supervision. Students can also use Submit revision to record a saved course revision, but those counts and timestamps do not include map content. Use the shared evidence and Field Notes to frame one question for the next revision.

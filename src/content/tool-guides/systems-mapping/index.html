About Systems Mapping

Systems Mapping helps students map how people, settings, and decisions connect. Starting with a research memo, students build a network graph that links everyday relationships and local settings to the institutions, policies, and broader historical and social conditions that shape them. They can trace how a decision made by a school, public agency, or funding body affects people in a community, and how those effects connect across different parts of the map.

The project draws on ecological systems theory and asset mapping to examine both the barriers people face and the resources they draw on, including community organizations, informal networks, and local knowledge. Students use evidence from their research to decide what belongs on the map, which connections need further investigation, and where there may be opportunities for community advocacy.

Maria Mavrides Calderon proposed Systems Mapping for a course on the systems that shape the lives of young people and families in New York City. It was developed through the Critical AI Literacy Institute, and the CUNY AI Lab hosts it.

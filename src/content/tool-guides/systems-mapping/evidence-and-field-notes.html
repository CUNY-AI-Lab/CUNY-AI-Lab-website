Evidence Records

Each item and relationship can hold an evidence record. Quote the relevant sentence or paragraph in Source passage, then use Claim and reasoning to explain what you think the passage shows. Keeping those fields separate lets a reader compare the source with your interpretation.

Use Uncertainty for a rating or sentence about what you still need to establish. Counterevidence holds a passage that challenges your claim, while Missing perspectives names someone whose view the memo leaves out. A record can therefore show why you kept an item and what could change your mind.

The app checks that references exist and required fields follow the expected structure. Students and instructors judge whether a passage supports a claim. A completed field alone cannot settle that question.

Field Notes

Each level asks you to answer one question your instructor publishes. The default question asks what you noticed at the current level and which items could help you begin the next one. A response is required to complete the level. Your draft saves immediately, and you can return to the Map at any point while writing.

Earlier notes remain available in collapsed sections. Your answers stay intact if the instructor changes a question, and the next level's memo review receives your previous Field Notes as your own notes, separate from the memo.

Writing Field Notes That Help

A useful Field Note names a gap and a way to investigate it. For example, a student might write, “I have no evidence for who decides Rosa's appointment times.” That sentence directs attention to an outside decision setting the student can investigate at Exo. If the memo comes from fieldwork, describe what you would observe or ask at the next visit.

Refer to map items by label so another reader can find them in a shared map or exported copy. A note about “Rosa's appointment times” gives the next conversation a clearer starting point than a general claim that the system needs more detail. Submit revision records course progress, but does not send the instructor your Field Notes or map content.

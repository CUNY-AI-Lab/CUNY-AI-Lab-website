Adding Evidence

As you add people, organizations, and relationships to your map, you can connect each one to a passage from your memo. Put the passage in Source passage, then use Claim and reasoning to explain what you think it shows.

If you are unsure about a claim or need to learn more, describe that under Uncertainty. You can also add a passage that challenges the claim under Counterevidence and name views the memo leaves out under Missing perspectives.

Read the passage in context and consider whether it supports what you have put on the map.

Field Notes

Each level asks you to answer one question your instructor publishes. The default question asks what you noticed at the current level and which items could help you begin the next one. A response is required to complete the level. Your draft saves immediately, and you can return to the Map at any point while writing.

You can open earlier notes in the collapsed sections, and your answers remain there even if your instructor changes a question. When the app reads the memo for the next level, it also receives your earlier Field Notes as your own notes, separate from the memo.

Writing Field Notes

You can use Field Notes to write down what you want to learn next. For example, if a fictional memo describes Rosa's hospital visits, you might ask who decides when her appointments take place and explore that question at Exo.

When you refer to an item on your map, use its label so someone reading a shared map or exported copy can find it. Choosing Submit revision records your course progress, but it does not send your instructor the map or your Field Notes.

Evidence Records

You can add evidence to each item and relationship. Quote a sentence or paragraph in Source passage, then use Claim and reasoning to explain what you think it shows.

Use Uncertainty to describe what you still need to find out. Add a passage that challenges your claim under Counterevidence, and use Missing perspectives to name views the memo leaves out.

Read the source and decide whether it supports your claim. Filling in a field does not make a claim accurate.

Field Notes

Each level asks you to answer one question your instructor publishes. The default question asks what you noticed at the current level and which items could help you begin the next one. A response is required to complete the level. Your draft saves immediately, and you can return to the Map at any point while writing.

Earlier notes remain available in collapsed sections. Your answers stay intact if the instructor changes a question, and the next level's memo review receives your previous Field Notes as your own notes, separate from the memo.

Writing Field Notes That Help

Use a Field Note to name something you want to find out. For example, if your memo is about Rosa's hospital visits, you might write, “I have no evidence for who decides Rosa's appointment times.” You can then investigate that question at Exo.

Refer to map items by label so another reader can find them in a shared map or exported copy. A note about “Rosa's appointment times” gives the next conversation a clearer starting point than a general claim that the system needs more detail. Submit revision records course progress, but does not send the instructor your Field Notes or map content.

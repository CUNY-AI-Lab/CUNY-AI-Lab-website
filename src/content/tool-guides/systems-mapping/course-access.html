Workspaces

When you sign in to Systems Mapping, you can use the general workspace or choose a course workspace for a course you have joined. Each workspace keeps its own maps, links, and prompts, so a map you save in one will not move when you open another.

Students can choose a course under Your courses on the Projects page or follow a link from their instructor. The app checks enrollment, so students who leave a course lose access even if they still have the link. Course owners can use the instructor controls for courses they own; owning one course does not give them access to another.

What an Instructor Sees

ItemVisible to the course instructor?
Roster and first/latest visitYes, from admission records and the course workspace.
Saved progress and submitted revisionsYes. The instructor sees progress, counts, and timestamps, but not the map itself.
Map content, memo, evidence, Field NotesThrough an eligible project link shared with them, not through Submit revision. You can also share an exported copy separately.
Map content without a shared link or copyNo. Recording a revision does not give the instructor access to the map.
General-workspace mapsNot through the instructor role. General-workspace project-link rules still apply.

Instructors can use the roster to see who has started and how far they have gotten. When students choose Submit revision, the app records their progress but does not send you their maps, so ask for a project link or an exported copy if you want to read them. The separate Submissions list may include reviews from an earlier workflow.

Guest Instructors

A guest instructor can be given permission to open the instructor panel, read the roster, or read and edit prompts without being a course owner, student, or administrator. The app checks these permissions on each request.

Demo Course

The CAIL Teaching Tools Demo course supports workshops and try-outs. The Lab issues revocable invitations tied to verified CUNY identities. Demo maps follow the same privacy rules as maps in other courses.

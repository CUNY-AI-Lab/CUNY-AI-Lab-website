These activities ask students to work with a memo, build a map, and write Field Notes. An empty map and a new level without existing work require an initial reading of the memo. Afterward, students can edit their work without requesting another reading.

1. Memo to Map

Begin at Micro with a shared one-page case and Automatic suggestion addition off. Create a project, name the person in your question, and paste the memo. Before choosing Generate guidance, list the people and places you expect to find. Compare the suggestions with that list and the memo.

Use Add item to add something the model missed. Record a passage and explain how it supports the item. Add relationships at Meso. If a student adds a hospital billing office at Micro, ask whether the person in the memo participates there. A decision made elsewhere can be mapped at Exo.

2. Sort the Suggestions

Open Details before deciding on each suggestion. Check whether it cites a passage and whether that passage supports the label. Add supported suggestions, set aside inferences that need evidence, and remove those the memo contradicts.

Choose a suggestion you removed and explain why you removed it. Compare your choices with a partner who read the same memo, and look at the passage together if you disagree.

3. Connect the Micro Map

At Meso, choose two items you added at Micro. Select the first, choose Connect from [node name], and select the second. Name the relationship, choose its direction, and add evidence from the memo. Then answer the Meso Field Notes question.

For a clinic-to-school arrow labeled “calls,” ask what the call carries. “Sends appointment reminders to” is more informative when the source supports it. Choose a two-way arrow only when the evidence supports both directions.

4. Follow the Decision

After Part 1, add a reading and begin Exo. Find a decision made elsewhere that affects the person in the memo, such as a schedule change or closed program. Add the place or group that made the decision and connect it to a setting at Micro. Explain the connection using the reading.

If you use Check this connection, compare its notes with your evidence before revising. The result should make the indirect effect easier for another reader to trace.

5. Connect a Broader Condition

After Exo, use a policy summary to identify a law, policy, economic condition, or belief for Macro. Connect it to at least two earlier items and explain each connection using the reading. Export a PNG for discussion.

Distinguish a policy from the office applying it. An attendance law can appear at Macro, while an education office can appear at Exo when its decisions affect the person in the memo.

6. Field Notes Exchange

Pair students in the same course using maps that have reached Meso or later. Share project links and open a partner's map in List. Find a relationship that needs a passage and an item whose level you question. Explain both concerns and one thing the map helped you notice.

Use the exchange to revise and save your own map. Record a course revision if requested; that record tracks progress, while the live link shares the updated content.

Adapting the Exercises

For asynchronous teaching, use shared links or exports and your course's feedback channel. In a large section, begin with Memo to Map and Sort the Suggestions on a shared case before moving to fieldwork.

Leave time to review model output rather than assuming a fixed completion time. A saved guidance request may need to be checked again later; Privacy & Limits explains its budgets and retries. Reading and editing existing saved work makes no model request. Ask students to explain one decision before requesting another pass.

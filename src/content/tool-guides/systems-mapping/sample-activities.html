These activities give students ways to work with a memo, build a map, and use Field Notes to consider what they want to investigate. The app must first read the memo for a new map and for each new level without existing work; after that, students can edit without requesting another reading.

1. Memo to Map

With a short case that everyone has read, students can start by naming the person they want to understand and listing the people and places they expect to find. With Automatic suggestion addition off, they can create a project, paste the memo, and compare their lists with the suggestions that appear after they choose Generate guidance.

Students can use Add item for anything the model missed, record a passage that supports it, and add relationships at Meso. If they are unsure where to place an organization, they can consider whether the person in the memo participates there or is affected by decisions made elsewhere.

2. Sort the Suggestions

Open Details before deciding on each suggestion. Check whether it cites a passage and whether that passage supports the label. Add supported suggestions, set aside inferences that need evidence, and remove those the memo contradicts.

Choose a suggestion you removed and explain your decision to a partner who read the same memo. If you disagree, return to the passage together and consider what it supports.

3. Connect the Micro Map

At Meso, choose two items you added at Micro. Select the first, choose Connect from [node name], and select the second. Name the relationship, choose its direction, and add evidence from the memo. Then answer the Meso Field Notes question.

When the source gives enough detail, students can use a label that describes what happens between the two items. For example, “sends appointment reminders to” says more than “calls”; the evidence can also help them decide whether the arrow should point one way or both.

4. Follow the Decision

After Part 1, students might read about a decision made elsewhere that affects the person in the memo, such as a schedule change or a program closing. At Exo, they can add the place or group that made the decision, connect it to a setting at Micro, and use the reading to explain the connection.

If students use Check this connection, they can compare its notes with their evidence before deciding whether to revise.

5. Connect a Broader Condition

After Exo, use a policy summary to identify a law, policy, economic condition, or belief for Macro. Connect it to at least two earlier items and explain each connection using the reading. Export a PNG for discussion.

Distinguish a policy from the office applying it. An attendance law can appear at Macro, while an education office can appear at Exo when its decisions affect the person in the memo.

6. Field Notes Exchange

Students in the same course can share project links and look at each other's maps in List once they have reached Meso or later. They might talk about a relationship that needs more evidence, an item whose placement is uncertain, or something the map helped them notice.

After the exchange, students can revise and save their own maps. If an instructor requests a course revision, the record tracks progress, while the live link shares the updated map.

Adapting the Exercises

For asynchronous teaching, use shared links or exports and your course's feedback channel. In a large section, begin with Memo to Map and Sort the Suggestions on a shared case before moving to fieldwork.

Leave time for students to review the suggestions, since a guidance request may need to be checked again later. Privacy & Limits explains the request limits and retries. Students can read and edit existing saved work without making another request.

Use these exercises within the memo → map → Field Notes assignment. An empty map and a new level without existing work require an initial memo reading. Afterward, students can work manually; later rereading and Map Check are optional unless the exercise examines their output.

1. Memo to Map

Begin at Micro with a shared one-page case and Automatic suggestion addition off. Create a project, name the focal person in the inquiry, and paste the memo. Before choosing Generate guidance, list the people and places you expect to find. Compare any suggestions with that list and the source.

Use Add item for a supported item the model missed. Record a source passage and interpretation for each item; relationships come at Meso. If a student adds a hospital billing office at Micro, ask whether the focal person participates there. An outside decision setting belongs at Exo when its effect is indirect.

2. Sort the Suggestions

Open Details before deciding on each suggestion. Check whether it cites a passage and whether that passage supports the label. Add supported suggestions, set aside inferences that need evidence, and remove those the memo contradicts.

Choose one removed suggestion and explain what it claimed, what the source says, and why the model may have produced it. Compare choices with a partner who read the same memo. Return to the passage when you disagree about wording, placement, or inference.

3. Connect the Micro Map

Start at Meso with at least three mapped Micro items. Find a supported relationship, select its starting item, choose Connect from [node name], and select the other item. Give the relationship a verb or phrase, choose its direction, and record the evidence. Use uncertainty rather than inventing support, then answer Meso Field Notes.

For a clinic-to-school arrow labeled “calls,” ask what the call carries. “Sends appointment reminders to” is more informative when the source supports it. Choose a two-way arrow only when the evidence supports both directions.

4. Follow the Decision

After Part 1, add a short institutional reading and begin Exo. Find a decision that affects the focal person without their participation, such as a schedule change or closed program. Map the outside decision setting and trace its pathway into a Micro setting. Explain frequency, conditions, and change over time.

If you use Check this connection, compare its notes with your evidence before revising. The result should make the indirect effect easier for another reader to trace.

5. Scope the Condition

After Exo, use a policy summary to identify one law, policy, economic condition, or belief for Macro. Scope it to at least two earlier items, explain each scope through evidence, and add counterevidence about where it may not apply. Export a PNG for discussion.

Keep the condition distinct from the office applying it. State attendance law is a Macro condition; a Department of Education office may be an Exo decision setting, depending on the case.

6. Field Notes Exchange

Pair students in the same course using maps that have reached Meso or later. Share project links and open a partner's map in List. Find a relationship that needs a passage and an item whose level you question. Explain both concerns and one thing the map helped you notice.

Use the exchange to revise and save your own map. Record a course revision if requested; that record tracks progress, while the live link shares the updated content.

Adapting the Exercises

For asynchronous teaching, use shared links or exports and your course's feedback channel. In a large section, begin with Memo to Map and Sort the Suggestions on a shared case before moving to fieldwork.

Leave time to review model output rather than assuming a fixed completion time. A saved guidance request may need to be checked again later; Privacy & Limits explains its budgets and retries. Reading and editing existing saved work makes no model request. Ask students to explain one decision before requesting another pass.

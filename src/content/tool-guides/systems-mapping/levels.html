The Progression

The four levels help you look at a situation from different distances. For example, if a fictional memo follows Rosa to a hospital appointment, the hospital belongs at Micro because she goes there; its billing office might belong at Exo if its decisions affect her without her participation.

Start with the people, places, and other details in the memo, then connect them at Meso. Exo brings in decisions made elsewhere, and Macro makes room for broader conditions such as policies and economic circumstances. Relationship fields also let you describe how a connection changes over time.

Part 1, Micro and Meso

Micro

Map the people and places connected to the person in the memo. You can also add groups, roles, activities, objects, and resources when the memo supports them. For a memo about a hospital visit, you might add the person, a nurse, and the hospital as separate items, with evidence for each. Add relationship arrows at Meso.

Meso

Select a Micro item, choose Connect from [node name] in its details, then select a different Micro item. Name the relationship, record evidence, and choose a one-way or two-way arrow. For example, Nurse Jo “refers” Rosa; the hospital may exchange paperwork with the neighborhood center. Let the source establish direction rather than assuming both ways.

Part 2, Exo and Macro

Exo

Map a place or group whose decisions affect the person without their participation, such as a billing office or a parent's employer. Show how the decision affects a setting at Micro. Add another office between them when the memo supports that step.

Macro

Identify a broader condition, such as a law, policy, economic condition, history, or belief. Connect it to at least two earlier items and explain each connection using the memo. Place an organization according to its role in the case; use Macro for the broader condition.

How Work Carries Forward

The map builds on the work you have already done: items stay in place when you connect them at Meso, and at Macro you can link a broader condition to items from earlier levels. At Meso and Exo, you can also describe how a relationship changes over time.

Before you continue from Meso, the Review Micro and Meso dialog lets you choose Review the map or Continue to Exo-level. Check for omissions and unsupported relationships before continuing. A new level without work needs its own reading once course guidance is ready; existing saved maps remain available without regeneration.

When you open an older map, the app moves earlier Micro relationships to Meso.

Common Placement Questions

A classroom can appear at Micro for a student who attends it. A district office can appear at Exo when an attendance decision affects that student indirectly. A parent's workplace can also affect a child, for example when a shift change alters the family's schedule.

You might use Macro for housing insecurity and Exo for a housing court or landlord whose decision affects the person in the memo. At Meso, connect two items from Micro and use evidence to explain the relationship and its direction.

The CUNY AI Lab (CAIL) is a faculty- and staff-led initiative at the CUNY Graduate Center that builds and maintains AI infrastructure for teaching, research, and academic work across CUNY. Its work involves hosting and maintaining the [CAIL Sandbox](https://chat.ailab.gc.cuny.edu/), where faculty, staff, and students can engage with curated open-weight models, configure tools for research and classroom use, and consult a public [Model Registry](/models) that explains model capabilities, licensing, and recommended use cases. The CAIL Model API is stateless and uses zero-data-retention model-provider paths; it does not store chat history. The Sandbox normally saves conversations to the user's account. Do not submit sensitive or regulated data without institutional approval. CAIL's aim is not simply to provide access to AI, but to make these systems legible enough for users to evaluate, adapt, and question the tools they use.

Alongside the Sandbox, CAIL develops [tools](/tools) for media processing and research workflows. Current projects include transcription, image description, OCR, research support, and website development. These projects are not yet available through CAIL Doorway. Each tool's storage and processing behavior can differ, and external processing is separate from any model-provider setting. Do not submit sensitive or regulated data without institutional approval. Inside the Sandbox, the Lab also develops classroom chatbots, course-specific assistants built from syllabi and readings, and reusable project templates that instructors can adapt across sections and semesters.

CAIL also supports teaching and experimentation through documentation, training, events, and reusable technical infrastructure. The Lab publishes [Sandbox documentation](https://cuny-ai-lab.github.io/sandbox-docs/), [guides and tutorials](/guides); hosts [events](/events); and shares its open-source projects on [GitHub](https://github.com/CUNY-AI-Lab) for reuse and adaptation.

Our work is guided by privacy, sustainability, transparency, and critical AI literacy. In practice, this means documenting provider-specific settings for model requests and responses, separating those settings from app storage and external tool processing, attending to environmental cost, using open-weight models that can be audited, and emphasizing literacy and judgment rather than dependency.

The Lab draws on recent work such as the [Google.org-sponsored Critical AI Literacy Institute (CALI)](https://criticalai.commons.gc.cuny.edu/), as well as initiatives like the [CUNY Academic Commons](https://commons.gc.cuny.edu/) and [Manifold Scholarship](https://cuny.manifoldapp.org/), which reflect the Graduate Center's longstanding commitment to creating accessible, scalable digital infrastructure for and with CUNY faculty, students, and staff.

CAIL is jointly administered by [Graduate Center Digital Initiatives (GCDI)](https://gcdi.commons.gc.cuny.edu/), the [Teaching and Learning Center (TLC)](https://tlc.commons.gc.cuny.edu/), the [Mina Rees Library](https://library.gc.cuny.edu/), and the [American Social History Project / Center for Media and Learning (ASHP/CML)](https://ashp.cuny.edu/).

title: Grant Proposals
---

If you're planning a grant proposal that involves the CUNY AI Lab, we'd be glad to work with you. You might want to name the Lab as a partner, use our tools or Model API in the project, budget for model use, or ask us for a letter of support. Please talk with us before you name the Lab, its staff, or its services in a proposal.

CAIL is a small team, and much of our time goes to the services, workshops, and consultations we already offer. While the Lab is in its pilot phase, access is open to everyone at the Graduate Center and to selected faculty and staff from other campuses; students elsewhere at CUNY can only join as part of a class pilot or project. If we talk early, we can tell you what we're able to commit to for the length of the grant. We can also check the details that are easy to get wrong from outside the Lab, such as which models we run, how we handle data, what model use is likely to cost, and how much of our staff time you'd need.

## When to get in touch

As early as you can. If you need a letter of support, please write to us at least four weeks before the funder's deadline, and earlier if you'd like help shaping the project or its budget. Your campus's sponsored programs office will usually set its own internal deadline a week or two before the funder's, so plan around the campus deadline too.

## What to send us

Email [ailab@gc.cuny.edu](mailto:ailab@gc.cuny.edu) with "Grant proposal" in the subject line, and include:

- the funder and program, with a link to the call;
- the funder's deadline, and your campus's internal deadline if you know it;
- a paragraph or two about the project and who is on the team;
- what you hope the Lab will contribute; and
- what data you'll work with, especially if it includes student records, research participants, health information, or other sensitive material.

You don't need a finished draft before you write to us.

## What we can help with

Depending on the project and our capacity, we can:

- talk through the AI parts of the project, including whether AI is the right tool for them;
- write a letter of support or collaboration;
- describe the Lab's tools and infrastructure for a facilities or resources section;
- estimate model-use costs for your budget, using the token prices in our [Model Registry](/models/);
- answer questions about how we handle data, for a data management plan or IRB protocol; and
- plan workshops or training for the project team.

## Beyond our standard access

Without additional resources, we can offer only our standard access, as the FAQ describes it: [who can use the Lab](/faq/#who-can-use) and the [standard allowance](/faq/#is-it-free) of $5 per month of model use.

If your project needs more than that, we can take it on only if the proposal commits additional resources to the Lab to cover it. That includes more people or campuses than we currently admit, a higher spending allowance, dedicated capacity, custom development, hosting, or sustained staff time. We need to agree on those resources with you before you submit.

## What we can't promise

- Please don't list a Lab staff member as a co-PI, key personnel, or consultant until the staff member has agreed to the role.
- We can't host or maintain project software after the grant ends unless we've agreed to do so in advance.
- We work only with open-weight models. Our Model API serves them, and requests to providers are configured for zero retention. We can't support a project that depends on a closed or commercial model or AI service, such as a proposal built around a specific proprietary chatbot or API. Please plan the project around open-weight models. We can help you choose one, and our [Model Registry](/models/) lists what's available.
- Our cloud tools aren't currently offered for identifiable human-subject research data or other regulated data, such as FERPA- or HIPAA-protected records or identifiable material covered by an IRB protocol. If your project involves data like this, please contact us before you plan around our tools.

## Projects we've partnered on

Some of the projects we've worked on with CUNY faculty:

- **Building Critical AI Literacies in City College Humanities Courses.** Isabel Estrada, Professor of Iberian and Latin American Cultures at City College, leads this project, funded by the CUNY AI Innovation Fund, with Stefano Morello and Zach Muhlbauer of the Lab. In spring 2026, its first cohort of four City College humanities faculty designed course assistants and assignments in the CAIL Sandbox with our help.
- **CUNY SPH AI Field Guide: Coding Agents for Public Health Teaching and Learning.** Levi Waldron, Distinguished Professor and Chair of Epidemiology and Biostatistics at the CUNY Graduate School of Public Health and Health Policy, submitted this proposal to the CUNY AI Innovation Fund in fall 2026, with Stefano Morello of the Lab as a collaborator. It has not been decided yet. The team plans 10 to 15 short, open teaching materials on coding agents in public health courses and has budgeted for the project's model use through the Lab.
- **[Critical AI Literacy Institute (CALI)](https://criticalai.commons.gc.cuny.edu/).** The Google.org-sponsored CALI is the Graduate Center Teaching and Learning Center's faculty-development program. Faculty in its T(h)inkering with AI track build a tool in the CAIL Sandbox for a specific course problem, and we provide Sandbox access, model configuration support, and technical coaching. In spring 2026, five tools from the second cohort reached working state: a genre-listening tutor for students with no music background (Lehman College); a qualitative-research assistant that helps novice researchers read ethnographic field notes without analyzing the data for them (City College); a physics-lab scaffolding tool for a non-majors course (Queensborough Community College); a systems-and-assets mapping assistant grounded in ecological systems theory (Hunter College); and a precalculus tool that ties concepts to each student's field (York College).
- **[Stem Splitter](/stem-splitter-docs/).** Agustina Checa proposed this tool for teaching close listening in music courses. Zach Muhlbauer prototyped and developed it through CALI.
- **[Systems Mapping](/systems-mapping-docs/).** Maria Mavrides Calderon proposed this tool for a course on the systems that shape the lives of young people and families in New York City. It was also developed through CALI.
- **[Translating Shakespeare](/blog/translating-shakespeare/).** Ellen Siyuan Pan created a custom model in the CAIL Sandbox for her introductory Shakespeare class, seeded with deliberate errors for students to find.

## If your proposal is funded

Let us know, and we'll set up a time to plan the work. When you publish or present the project, please credit the CUNY AI Lab for the tools, infrastructure, or support you used. We'd also like to hear how it goes.

title: Collaborations and Grant Proposals
---

The CUNY AI Lab is open to collaborating on projects that align with our scope and goals. If you're considering a grant proposal that involves the Lab, please reach out while the project is still in the planning phase. Before you include the Lab's staff or services in a proposal, please discuss with us roles, funding, and what the project would need from us.

We're especially interested in partnering on projects across the disciplines that center human autonomy and decision-making, and that use AI to augment human labor and agency, rather than replace them. We also look for projects that share [the principles that guide our own work](/about/), including sustainability, transparency, and critical AI literacy.

CAIL is run by a small team, and much of our time goes to the services, workshops, and consultations we already offer. While the Lab is in its pilot phase, access is open to all Graduate Center faculty, staff, and graduate students, and to selected faculty, staff, and graduate students from other CUNY campuses. An early conversation allows us to plan together what resources we'll need to support your project. It also helps you describe our services accurately in your proposal, including which models we run, how we run them, how we handle data, and what staff support the Lab can and cannot provide.

## How to reach us

Tell us about the project through our [grant proposal form](https://tools.ailab.gc.cuny.edu/help/projects/support/issues/new?topic=grant) in CAIL Ticketing, where you sign in with your CUNY Login. If you can't sign in, email [ailab@gc.cuny.edu](mailto:ailab@gc.cuny.edu).

If you need a letter of support, please contact us at least four weeks before the funder's deadline, and earlier if you'd like help shaping the project or its budget. Your campus's Sponsored Research office will usually set its own internal deadline a week or two before the funder's, so plan around their deadline too.

## Limits

- Please don't list a Lab staff member as a co-PI, personnel, or consultant until the staff member has agreed to the role.
- We can't host, build, or maintain ad-hoc software for a project, during the grant or after it, unless we've agreed to do so in advance.
- We can't make exceptions to our access policy or usage allowance. Anything beyond [who can use the Lab](/faq/#who-can-use) and the [standard allowance](/faq/#is-it-free) needs additional resources committed to the Lab, agreed before you submit your grant application.
- Our computing capacity is very limited. We can't promise dedicated capacity, large-scale processing, or model training for a project. We can help you budget for it, or work out where the project could get more, depending on its needs and the resources available.
- We work only with open-weight models configured for zero data retention. We can't support a project that depends on a closed or commercial model or AI service, such as a proposal built around a specific proprietary chatbot or API. See our [Model Registry](/models/) for the models we currently offer.
- Our cloud tools aren't currently offered for identifiable human-subject research data or other regulated data, such as FERPA- or HIPAA-protected records or identifiable material covered by an IRB protocol. If your project involves data like this, we may be able to offer on-premises processing on Graduate Center hardware.

## Current and past collaborations

- **Building Critical AI Literacies in City College Humanities Courses.** With Isabel Estrada at City College, funded by the CUNY AI Innovation Fund. In spring 2026, four humanities faculty designed course assistants and assignments in the CAIL Sandbox.
- **CUNY SPH AI Field Guide.** With Levi Waldron and Jennifer Majer at the CUNY Graduate School of Public Health and Health Policy, funded by the CUNY AI Innovation Fund. The team is writing open teaching materials on using coding agents in public health courses.
- **AI for What?!?** With Katie Cumiskey and the CUNY Public Interest Technology (PIT) Lab, funded by the Robin Hood Learning + Technology Fund. In spring 2026, we supported a graduate course that enabled 11 students to work with 9 NYC non-profits on critical AI practice. CAIL offered class visits, workshops, and technical consultations. This collaboration led to the development of PIT Lab's [GitHub organization](https://github.com/CUNY-PIT-Lab), an [AI website guide and evaluation suite](https://github.com/CUNY-PIT-Lab/sidecar-website-guide) with Jacob Schwartz (Fortune Society) and DH MA students Maria Baker and Sasha Richardson, along with a lab protocol model prototyped in the CAIL Sandbox with Boryana Ivanova and Casey Lardner (Genspace).
- **[Critical AI Literacy Institute (CALI)](https://criticalai.commons.gc.cuny.edu/).** With the Graduate Center's Teaching and Learning Center. Faculty fellows in CALI's T(h)inkering with AI track begin from problems their students face in a course, then work with us to develop, test, and refine AI-supported interventions within their own classes. Some became standalone apps, such as [Stem Splitter](https://tools.ailab.gc.cuny.edu/stem-splitter/) for close listening in music courses and [Systems Mapping](https://tools.ailab.gc.cuny.edu/systems-mapping/) for a course on the systems that shape young people's lives, while others serve as custom AI models built to support CUNY students with sociological fieldwork, structured Physics lab reports, and interdisciplinary learning in precalculus coursework.

title: Collaborations and Grant Proposals
---

If you're considering a grant proposal that involves the CUNY AI Lab, we'd be glad to work with you. Before you include the Lab, its staff, or its services in a proposal, please talk with us about roles, funding, and what the project would need from us.

CAIL is run by a small team, and much of our time goes to the services, workshops, and consultations we already offer. While the Lab is in its pilot phase, access is open to all Graduate Center faculty, staff, and graduate students, and to selected faculty, staff, and graduate students from other CUNY campuses. An early conversation lets us plan together what resources we'll need to support your project. It also helps you describe our services accurately in the proposal: which models we run and how we run them, how we handle data, and what staff support the Lab can and cannot provide.

## How to reach us

Tell us about the project through our [grant proposal form](https://tools.ailab.gc.cuny.edu/help/projects/support/issues/new?topic=grant) in CAIL Ticketing, where you sign in with your CUNY Login. You don't need a finished draft. If you can't sign in, email [ailab@gc.cuny.edu](mailto:ailab@gc.cuny.edu).

If you need a letter of support, please write to us at least four weeks before the funder's deadline, and earlier if you'd like help shaping the project or its budget. Your campus's sponsored programs office will usually set its own internal deadline a week or two before the funder's, so plan around the campus deadline too.

## Limits to plan around

- Please don't list a Lab staff member as a co-PI, key personnel, or consultant until the staff member has agreed to the role.
- We can't host, build, or maintain software for a project, during the grant or after it, unless we've agreed to do so in advance.
- We can't make exceptions to our access policy or usage allowance. Anything beyond [who can use the Lab](/faq/#who-can-use) and the [standard allowance](/faq/#is-it-free) needs additional resources committed to the Lab, agreed before you submit.
- Our computing capacity is very limited. We can't promise dedicated capacity, large-scale processing, or model training for a project. We can help you budget for it, or work out where the project could get more, depending on its needs and the resources available.
- We work only with open-weight models configured for zero data retention. We can't support a project that depends on a closed or commercial model or AI service, such as a proposal built around a specific proprietary chatbot or API. See our [Model Registry](/models/) for the models we currently offer.
- Our cloud tools aren't currently offered for identifiable human-subject research data or other regulated data, such as FERPA- or HIPAA-protected records or identifiable material covered by an IRB protocol. If your project involves data like this, we may be able to offer on-premises processing on Graduate Center hardware. Please contact us.

## Projects we've partnered on

- **Building Critical AI Literacies in City College Humanities Courses.** With Isabel Estrada at City College, funded by the CUNY AI Innovation Fund. In spring 2026, four humanities faculty designed course assistants and assignments in the CAIL Sandbox.
- **CUNY SPH AI Field Guide.** With Levi Waldron and Jennifer Majer at the CUNY Graduate School of Public Health and Health Policy, funded by the CUNY AI Innovation Fund. The team is writing open teaching materials on using coding agents in public health courses.
- **[Critical AI Literacy Institute (CALI)](https://criticalai.commons.gc.cuny.edu/).** With the Graduate Center's Teaching and Learning Center. Faculty in CALI's T(h)inkering with AI track bring a problem from one of their own courses, and we build a tool for it with them. Some became standalone apps, such as [Stem Splitter](/stem-splitter-docs/) for close listening in music courses and [Systems Mapping](/systems-mapping-docs/) for a course on the systems that shape young people's lives. Others include custom AI models built in the CAIL Sandbox to support CUNY students with qualitative fieldwork, structured lab reports, and interdisciplinary learning in mathematics education.

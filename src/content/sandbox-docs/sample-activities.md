The activities below are proposed adaptations of the documented designs in [Use Cases](use-cases.md). They are starting points to test, not reports of additional classroom implementations. Choose one that fits your assignment, prepare permitted source materials, and arrange [course access](student-onboarding.md) before inviting students.

## Check a Translation Against the Play

This adaptation draws on [Ellen Siyuan Pan's Shakespeare assignment](https://ailab.gc.cuny.edu/blog/translating-shakespeare/). Use a short passage whose language and context you can discuss with students. If your configuration deliberately introduces errors, explain that design in advance and test it; do not assume every error is intentional.

1. Read the passage before asking the course model for a modern-English translation. Mark a phrase whose meaning or tone needs attention.
2. Compare the translation with that phrase and its surrounding scene. Identify what the translation preserves, changes, or leaves out.
3. Ask the model about one disputed choice, then check its explanation against the play.
4. Revise the translation yourself and explain the evidence behind your change.

Keep the task focused on interpreting the source rather than spotting a fixed number of mistakes. A proposed multilingual adaptation can allow discussion in students' chosen languages while retaining a common translation language, as Pan's design did. ## Explain a Choice in an Experiment

Use the basic [STEM Adventure Games workshop configuration](https://github.com/CUNY-AI-Lab/sandbox-series/blob/45ffd2a3a42c54b4b8239a14d3b4225d82da0f28/examples/model-cards.md#stem-adventure-games) only after confirming access to the model and its attached sources. It is a text adventure with designed scenes and choices, not a physical experiment.

1. Choose an experiment from the opening menu.
2. At a procedural choice, explain what you expect to observe before selecting an option.
3. Compare the narrated result with your expectation. Ask the model to pause and show the source passage supporting its account.
4. Identify one documented detail and one invented or simplified part of the scene. Explain what the simulation cannot establish.

Review the quoted passage yourself. A game outcome does not prove that a historical experiment unfolded that way or that a student learned the concept. Keep a source-based alternative available if the model or collection cannot be accessed.

## Compare Two Revisions with Evidence

This adaptation follows the [Compare Wikipedia Edits workshop card](https://github.com/CUNY-AI-Lab/sandbox-series/blob/45ffd2a3a42c54b4b8239a14d3b4225d82da0f28/examples/model-cards.md#compare-wikipedia-edits). Before class, select two revisions you can open and verify, retaining their IDs, dates, and comparison link. Saved excerpts work when live retrieval is not needed.

1. Read a changed passage in both versions before asking for the model's comparison.
2. Decide whether it adds or removes a claim, changes a qualification or citation, or changes wording only.
3. Compare that judgment with the model's response. Check every quotation against the selected revisions.
4. Explain a disagreement or uncertainty using the quoted words, and identify any additional evidence needed.

Do not infer an editor's motives. Assess a citation change only when both versions' references are available; the presence of a new citation alone does not establish accuracy. In discussion, ask students to point to the wording that supports their judgment rather than offer general praise or criticism.<span id="what-to-watch-for-students-who-are-new-to-peer-review-often-default-to-vague-praise-good-job-or-surface-level-criticism-needs-more-detail-thats-a-starting-point-not-a-problem-use-the-step-5-comparison-to-help-them-see-what-more-specific-evidence-based-feedback-looks-like-in-practice" aria-hidden="true"></span>

## Close the Activity

Ask students to submit or discuss a permitted excerpt, the relevant source, and a brief explanation of one decision. Set the audience before sharing in a course channel; do not require unrelated private chat history. Use what students found to revise the instructions or configuration before the next activity.

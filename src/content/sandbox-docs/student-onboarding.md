Prepare one course activity before inviting students to explore the whole platform. They need a working login, the intended model, and a clear explanation of what to do with its response. Follow the sequence at the pace your class needs.

## Arrange Course Access<span id="instructor-checkpoint-complete-course-access-first" aria-hidden="true"></span>

Get course approval before setting up the class or inviting students. An individual account does not enroll your course.

1. Submit a [course access request](https://ailab.gc.cuny.edu/request-access/?kind=class). Select **A class I'm teaching or organizing**, sign in with **CUNY Login**, and provide the requested details.
2. After approval, open the course in [My classes](https://tools.ailab.gc.cuny.edu/my-classes), review its enrollment dates, and create or copy the invitation link.
3. Sign in to the [Sandbox](https://chat.ailab.gc.cuny.edu/) and confirm that the course's private group and text channel are available.
4. Share the invitation with students. They join through that link, not through individual access applications.

Check the roster in **My classes** before the activity. Contact [ailab@gc.cuny.edu](mailto:ailab@gc.cuny.edu) about unresolved enrollment or login problems.

## Prepare the Resources and Expectations

If you are creating a course model, request separate **Workspace** access from the Lab. Build and test the model before class, then keep its visibility **Private** and use **Add Access** to give the course group **Read** access. Do the same for attached knowledge collections, tools, and skills. Confirm that intended users can reach the resources; your owner view alone does not establish their access.

Tell students where AI use is permitted, what they must disclose, and which writing or analysis they must complete themselves. State what they should not upload, including personal information, confidential material, or another person's work without permission. Keep those expectations with the assignment rather than relying on the system prompt to enforce them.

Before class, try the task with teaching staff and check the interface and materials with the assistive technologies students will use. Arrange another way to complete the learning task if access or accessibility problems prevent participation.

## Walk Through Sign-In

Project the steps or share [Getting Started](getting-started.md), then allow time for students to resolve access issues:

1. Open the instructor's course invitation, sign in with **CUNY Login**, and confirm enrollment. Going directly to the Sandbox does not enroll someone in a course.
2. Open [chat.ailab.gc.cuny.edu](https://chat.ailab.gc.cuny.edu/) and choose **Continue with CUNY Login**. Authenticate again if prompted.
3. Check that the course's private text channel is available, then select the assigned model on the right inside the message box.

Enrollment and Sandbox sign-in add course group and channel membership automatically. If login stalls, try a private window or another browser. If course resources remain missing, ask the Lab for help before proceeding.

## Guide the First Chat<span id="day-1-guided-first-conversation-15-minutes" aria-hidden="true"></span>

Use a short task tied to material students have in front of them. Demonstrate entering the prompt, reading the response, and checking one claim or interpretation against that material. Then let students try the same sequence and ask a follow-up.

Ask what they accepted, challenged, or could not verify. Share an excerpt in the course channel only when that sharing fits the assignment and its audience is understood. [Sample Activities](sample-activities.md) offers proposed adaptations of documented designs for this step.

## Explain Chat Privacy

The model uses the current chat's history. If Memory is enabled or another chat is attached as context, information from outside the thread can also be included.

Administrators can access stored chats and system logs. Classmates see content shared with them or posted in the course channel. Provider requests are configured for zero retention, and using prompts or responses for model training is prohibited; saved Sandbox history is separate. Review [Privacy and Chat History](getting-started.md#privacy-and-chat-history) with students.

## Return to What Students Found

After the activity, discuss a response that needed checking and a decision the student made. Use those examples to revise the assignment, prompt, or document collection. Do not assume a polished output demonstrates understanding, or that a student who could not use the interface failed the learning task.
